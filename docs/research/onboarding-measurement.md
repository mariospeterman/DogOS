# Onboarding and Measurement Research Plan

- Status: research design, not a validated instrument
- Last reviewed: 2026-07-16

DogOS onboarding is a structured behavioral intake, not a dog personality score
and not a medical diagnostic instrument. It collects facts needed to choose and
measure one training goal with the fewest questions possible.

## Construct map

1. Dog identity: age band, sex/neuter status when relevant, size/body build,
   known breed or unknown/mixed, acquisition and history certainty.
2. Current context: household handlers, environment, routine, exercise,
   equipment actually used, and prior training.
3. Observable behavior: trigger, frequency, intensity, latency, distance,
   duration, recovery, and context. Avoid labels such as stubborn, dominant, or
   guilty.
4. Training variables: known cues, reinforcers, food/play engagement, handler
   timing/consistency, available environment, and feasible session length.
5. Goal baseline: one operational definition, observation window, known value
   or explicitly unknown value, and owner priority.
6. Event-triggered routing: sudden physical change, bite details, vulnerable
   person involvement, and ability to control distance. These questions route
   the affected exercise; they do not become a general health product.

## Instrument references

[C-BARQ](https://vetapps.vet.upenn.edu/cbarq/about.cfm) and the published short
and working-dog variants are research references for construct and item design,
not content to copy automatically. Permission, licensing, population fit, and
professional review are required before reusing instrument wording or claiming
comparability.

## Validation sequence

Follow COSMIN-style measurement development: content validity first, then
cognitive interviews, test-retest reliability, inter-rater agreement where two
handlers report the same dog, structural validity, internal consistency only
for reflective scales, criterion/known-groups evidence, and cross-language
measurement invariance.

German Swiss and English items must be separately reviewed. Locale, culture,
region, and handler gender may influence presentation and feasibility; they do
not change a canonical score or rule unless differential item functioning and
outcome validation support that change. Missing and “not observed” remain
distinct.

## Breed use

Breed is a low-weight prior for historical function, body build, maturation,
and plausible motivation hypotheses. It never overrides observed individual
behavior. Large genomic/behavior datasets report that breed explains only a
modest share of individual behavioral variation, so DogOS must validate every
breed-informed hypothesis against Milo's actual outcomes.

## Correlation and learning

- pre-register primary outcomes and adjustment hypotheses;
- separate exploratory correlations from confirmatory results;
- report effect sizes, uncertainty, missingness, and sample composition;
- validate calibration and performance on held-out households and time periods;
- test subgroup error and measurement invariance before regional adaptation;
- never display causal language from observational correlation;
- never create a universal dog score or public ranking.

## Data separation

Personal case memory remains household-scoped under RLS. The collective
knowledge graph contains sources, atomic claims, protocol versions, and
aggregate evaluation results, never raw conversations or identifiable case
histories. De-identified aggregate learning requires purpose limitation,
minimum cohort thresholds, consent/legal basis, deletion handling, and review.
No agent can promote its own discovered claim into a production protocol.
